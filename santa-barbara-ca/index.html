Q3 2026 Snapshot

Fewer deals, bigger bets,
a market still undersupplied.

South Coast sales slowed to 17 transactions in Q3, but two large deals — Google's $32.5M purchase of a Goleta campus and the $11.3M sale of Kellogg Square — drove 58% of the quarter's volume, and 71% of dollar volume traded off-market. Industrial and retail vacancy remain historically tight, Goleta office rents hit a record high, and State Street's available storefronts fell below 30 for the first time since 2019.

Rent & Vacancy by Sector

Conditions differ meaningfully across asset classes. Retail and industrial remain the tightest, while office continues its gradual recovery from pandemic-era softness.

Office Market

Santa Barbara office vacancy held relatively low in Q3 at 7.3%, up slightly from 6.9% in Q2. Yardi Systems' purchase of the former Macy's building at Paseo Nuevo for its future headquarters remains the defining downtown office story of 2026, while smaller expansions kept leasing moving — McCarthy & Kroes took 4,892 SF at 125 E Victoria St and Klein DeNatale added roughly 4,000 SF at 30 E Figueroa St. Several large blocks are still lingering, though: 614 Chapala St (27,217 SF) and 25 E Mason St (32,579 SF) have each been on the market for about three years.

Goleta posted the quarter's largest office lease — Toyon's 43,665 SF in the former AppFolio building at 50 Castilian Dr — and achieved rents reached a record $2.62/SF. More than 80,000 SF remains available across 7402, 6950, and 6550 Hollister Ave. In Carpinteria, reported vacancy jumped to 48.7% as two large vacant properties came to market: the 121,230 SF Enclave @ Carpinteria (former Procore campus) and the 40,763 SF building at 6185 Carpinteria Ave — together roughly a third of that submarket's office inventory.

Retail Market

Santa Barbara retail remains among the most constrained in the region. Citywide vacancy (excluding State Street) held at 3.0%, down from 3.8% a year ago, and the city logged 13 leases for the second straight quarter, with leased square footage rising to 34,654 SF. The largest was a 6,916 SF restaurant lease at 13–15 E Cabrillo Blvd (the former Blue Water Grill) for a new concept from the operators of Secret Bao; Columbia Bank also took 6,253 SF at 1035 State St and MarAmi Vineyard leased 3,890 SF at 136 E Haley St.

State Street's available storefronts fell to 29 — below 30 for the first time since 2019 — bringing availability down to 11.65%. The City formally adopted the State Street Master Plan during the quarter, though a voter referendum opposing the street's continued closure to cars has since qualified, keeping the corridor's future layout an open question. Premium environments — the Funk Zone, Montecito's Coast Village Road, and upper State Street — continue to see little turnover, with deals rarely publicly marketed. On the investment side, Kellogg Square in Goleta sold off-market for $11.3M (~$327/SF), with the buyer planning to remodel and keep it retail.

Industrial Market

Industrial remains the tightest segment in Santa Barbara County. Region-wide vacancy fell to 1.9% in Q3, down from 2.4% in Q2 and 2.7% a year ago, with 35,055 SF of positive net absorption — one of the most supply-constrained industrial markets in Southern California. Leasing itself was slow but steady: Goleta recorded one new lease, Santa Barbara very few, and the quarter included a handful of small renewals. A 77,394 SF project is now under construction in Goleta, the first meaningful new supply in some time.

After more than a decade of steady year-over-year increases, lease rates appear to be leveling off, giving tenants a bit more negotiating leverage. Larger spaces are the exception to the tightness: nine industrial spaces over 10,000 SF sat vacant across Santa Barbara, Goleta, and Carpinteria, many for extended periods. Smaller, well-located buildings still lease quickly.

Commercial Districts

Santa Barbara's Key Submarkets

Commercial real estate activity is concentrated in six distinct corridors, each with its own tenant mix, price point, and character.

Submarkets

Downtown / State Street
Historic commercial core. Mixed office, retail, and restaurant. Available storefronts fell below 30 in Q3 for the first time since 2019 (~11.7%). Yardi's HQ move into the former Macy's building and the housing conversion at the former Nordstrom building mark a turning point for two long-term vacancies, and the newly adopted State Street Master Plan now faces a voter referendum over the street's closure to cars.
Funk Zone
High-demand creative and hospitality district near the waterfront. Boutique retail, wineries, restaurants. Rents among the highest in the city; deals rarely public.
Montecito / Coast Village Road
Luxury neighborhood retail. Lowest vacancy and highest rents in the county. Near-zero turnover; strong demand from luxury and specialty tenants.
Goleta
Primary office and industrial corridor. Lower rents, more options. UCSB adjacency drives tech, biotech, and R&D tenants — Google's $32.5M Q3 campus purchase brings its local footprint to ~300,000 SF.
Carpinteria
Small-business industrial hub with a tight market. Attractive for cannabis, light manufacturing, and specialty production operations.
Lagoon District
Eastside mixed-use district bounded by Garden, Cota, Milpas, and Anacapa streets. Eclectic blend of commercial, light industrial, and creative tenants. An emerging alternative to State Street.

Mixed-Use · Eastside Santa Barbara

The Lagoon District

Bounded by Garden, Cota, Milpas, and Anacapa streets, the Lagoon District — named for the nearby Laguna Street — has quietly become one of Santa Barbara's most compelling commercial neighborhoods. Once known for light industrial and warehouse uses, the area now blends creative offices, breweries, specialty food producers, and boutique retail within the same eclectic blocks.

Property values and rents have risen steadily as the district has established its own identity. It offers a lower-cost, higher-character alternative to State Street, with a tenant mix that skews toward independent operators, design firms, and food and beverage concepts. Deals here move quickly and often don't reach public listings — making early market engagement especially valuable.
